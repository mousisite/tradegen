How we tested
  • Up to 5 years of daily prices for each stock and coin.
  • On {% if method.stride == 1 %}every trading day{% elif method.stride == 2 %}every other trading day{% elif method.stride == 3 %}every third trading day{% else %}one trading day in {{ method.stride }}{% endif %} on which the strategy leaned up or down, we took a pretend trade that way. Stop {{ method.stop }} ATR away, target {{ method.target }} ATR away, closed after {{ method.days }} trading days if it hit neither. ATR is how much the price usually moves in a day. Days tested counts those trades.
  • Worked means it reached the target before the stop. If both were touched on the same day, we count it as a loss.
  • Costs come off every trade: {{ method.equity }}% a round trip for stocks, {{ method.crypto }}% for crypto.
  • Kept a trade is in R, the amount risked. +0.10R means 10 cents kept for every dollar risked, on average, after costs.
  • The ± after it is the margin of error: the true figure lies inside it 95 times in 100, if the years ahead behave like the years tested. Trades a few days apart share most of their days and win or lose together, so the margin counts them as fewer, separate trades. Stocks that move together make even this margin a little narrow.
  • A strategy with fewer than {{ method.min }} separate trades on a stock is left out there: that few is luck.
  • The stocks and coins are today's popular names, picked after the years tested. A name often becomes popular by rising, so a strategy that mostly bought these can look better here than it would have at the time.
  • Over years when a stock rose, buying it on any day at all also kept money. So compare a strategy with buying on every tested day, the same pretend trade taken on all of them, not with zero: kept money alone does not show skill.

Re-run every day. Past results do not promise future ones. This is software output, not financial advice.
