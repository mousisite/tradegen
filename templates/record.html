How Orenth's calls turned out

These are real calls made at the time, scored with a pretend trade: no real money was traded.

A fingerprint of each day's calls

Once a day is over, the calls written down that day get a fingerprint: a SHA-256 hash of each call's stock, what it said, its price, stop and target, how many days it runs and when it was written. Change any of those by any amount, or add or remove a call, and the fingerprint changes. How to check one yourself

Download every call. Take that day's rows, the ones whose written_utc starts with its date, in the order the file lists them. Keep each row's first seven columns, symbol to horizon_days, exactly as the file has them, joined by commas, with a line break after each row. The SHA-256 of that text is the day's fingerprint. A call's score, and any reason it is left out, come in the columns after those, so scoring a call never changes its day's fingerprint. On a Mac or Linux, for {{ prints[0].start|day }}:

grep '^[^,]*,{{ prints[0].day }} ' orenth-record.csv | cut -d, -f1-7 | shasum -a 256

How it is scored
  • A stock's call is the first check after its market closes, at the closing price, and it is scored from the next trading day. A weekend or a holiday has no new close, so it has no new call. The page for each stock is checked through the day and can say something else in the afternoon; only the call after the close is scored.
  • A coin trades all day. Its call is the first check after midnight (UTC), at the price then, and it is scored from the next day's prices, so the rest of the day it was made is not counted. While the price source has not filled in the day before, checks write no call; the first check after it does writes it, at the price then. If the day is never filled in, that day has no call, and calls whose ten days hold it are left out, with the reason.
  • Then we pretend to buy at that price, with fixed levels: a stop {{ method.stop }} ATR below and a target {{ method.target }} ATR above, the rule the strategy tests use. ATR is how much the price usually moves in a day. A BUY's own plan can set its stop further away, below support, or its target nearer, at resistance; the record uses the fixed levels for every call, so every kind of call is scored by the same trade.
  • If it said BUY, it was right when the price reached the target before the stop, or after {{ method.days }} trading days had risen by at least half the distance to the stop. It was wrong when the stop was hit first, or the price had fallen by that much.
  • If it said WAIT or AVOID, the same pretend buy is scored the other way round: right when that buy would have lost, wrong when it would have made money. Because the stop is nearer than the target, a buy loses more often than it wins, so these are compared with chance, not with 50%.
  • A price that ended about where it started counts as neither.
  • A call counts in the scores once it is {{ rec.mature_days }} days old, or a day or two later around holidays, when every call made that day has had its full {{ method.days }} trading days. The calls that settle first are mostly the ones whose stop came quickly, so counting them sooner would favour calls that said not to buy.
  • The 95% range is how far the right rate could be from its true value. It allows for calls that are not separate tests, in two ways. The next day's call on a stock shares most of its {{ method.days }} days, so per stock a call counts as a new test only once the last one's days are over, and SPY and VOO, which hold the same companies, count as one. And on a day the whole market moves, calls on different stocks settle alike, so any two calls whose days overlap are counted as moving together, by as much as they actually did. The range given is the wider of the two. The second needs calls spread over about {{ rec.range_weeks }} weeks of the market; until then, no range is given. The average move after a kind of call has its range worked out the second way.
  • Moves are after trading costs: {{ method.equity }}% a round trip for stocks, {{ method.crypto }}% for crypto.

  • When a stock splits, a call made before the split is scored on its prices divided by the split, to match the history after it.

  • Calls are never edited or removed. One left out of the scores stays listed below, with the reason. Every call, left-out ones included, is in {% if in_app %}one file on the Orenth website that anyone can check{% else %}one file anyone can check{% endif %}{% if prints %}, and each day's calls have a fingerprint that an edit would change{% endif %}.

This is a forward test of software calls, not a record of real trades or money. Past results do not promise future ones. This is software output, not financial advice.
