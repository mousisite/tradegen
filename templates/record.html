How it is scored
  • Each stock's call is written down once per market quote, with the price at the time. A weekend or a holiday shows the last session's price again, so it does not count as a new call.
  • Then we pretend to buy at that price, with the same stop and target the app uses everywhere: {{ method.stop }} ATR below and {{ method.target }} ATR above. ATR is how much the price usually moves in a day.
  • If it said BUY, it was right when the price reached the target before the stop, or after {{ method.days }} trading days had risen by at least half the distance to the stop. It was wrong when the stop was hit first, or the price had fallen by that much.
  • If it said WAIT or AVOID, the same pretend buy is scored the other way round: right when that buy would have lost, wrong when it would have made money. Because the stop is nearer than the target, a buy loses more often than it wins, so these are compared with chance, not with 50%.
  • A price that ended about where it started counts as neither.
  • The 95% range is how far the right rate could be from its true value on this many calls. Calls on the same stock a day apart share most of their {{ method.days }} days, so they are not fully separate tests and the true range is wider.
  • Moves are after trading costs: {{ method.equity }}% a round trip for stocks, {{ method.crypto }}% for crypto.
  • Calls are never edited or removed. One left out of the scores stays listed below, with the reason.

This is a forward test of software calls, not a record of real trades or money. Past results do not promise future ones. This is software output, not financial advice.
